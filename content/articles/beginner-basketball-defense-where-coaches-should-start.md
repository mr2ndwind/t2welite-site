Defense is where many young basketball teams win or lose games, yet it is often the least taught part of the game. Before installing complex defensive schemes, coaches need to teach the fundamentals: stance, positioning, and on-ball defense. This guide explains where to start when teaching basketball defense to beginners.

## START WITH THE DEFENSIVE STANCE

Everything in defense starts with stance. If a player cannot get into and hold a proper defensive stance, no defensive concept will work. Teach stance before anything else.

- Feet shoulder-width apart, weight on the balls of the feet
- Knees bent, hips low — lower than the offensive player
- Back straight, chest up, hands active
- Head still, eyes on the offensive player's midsection

Have players hold the stance for 10-15 seconds at a time. If they cannot hold it, they need to build strength and endurance in the position before you can teach anything else.

## TEACH ON-BALL DEFENSE FIRST

On-ball defense is the foundation. The defender guarding the ball has one job: stay between the ball and the basket. Teach this before any help concepts.

- Stay between the ball and the basket
- Move the feet — do not cross over or reach
- Watch the midsection, not the ball or the eyes
- Keep hands active but controlled — no reaching

> FOR ATHLETES | Defense is played with the feet, not the hands. If you are reaching, you are beaten. Move your feet to stay in front and let your hands contest at the right moment.

## INTRODUCE CLOSEOUTS

A closeout is the transition from helping to guarding the ball. It is one of the hardest skills for young players. Teach it in stages: sprint to close the distance, then break down into a stance to contest.

## WHEN TO INTRODUCE HELP DEFENSE

Do not rush into help defense. If players cannot guard the ball, help concepts will create confusion. Once on-ball defense is solid, introduce the idea that the defender one pass away is in help position — ready to help if the on-ball defender gets beat.

## DEFENSIVE DRILLS FOR BEGINNERS

- Stance holds: 3 sets of 15 seconds in proper stance
- Mirror drill: one player moves, the other mirrors without crossing feet
- 1v1 closeout: offensive player catches, defender closes out and guards 1v1
- Shell drill (simplified): four defenders in a box, move with the ball

> KEY TAKEAWAY | Defense starts with stance. Teach the stance first, then on-ball defense, then closeouts, and only then introduce help concepts. Do not rush into complex schemes before the fundamentals are solid. Defense is played with the feet, not the hands.
