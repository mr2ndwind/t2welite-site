Pass, cut, and fill is the simplest motion offense concept and the best place to start teaching team offense. It creates movement, forces the defense to react, and gives every player a role. This guide explains how to teach pass, cut, and fill to young basketball players.

## THE THREE RULES

- Pass: move the ball to a teammate
- Cut: after passing, cut hard to the basket
- Fill: after cutting, fill the open spot on the perimeter

These three rules create constant movement without requiring players to memorize plays. The ball moves, players move, and the defense has to react.

## WHY CUTTING MATTERS

The cut is what makes this offense work. A hard cut to the basket after passing does three things: it gives the cutter a chance to receive a return pass for a layup, it forces the defender to follow and creates space, and it opens up the perimeter for the next pass.

> FOR ATHLETES | Cut to score, not just to move. Every cut should be at game speed with the expectation that you might get the ball. A jog to the basket is not a cut — it is a walk.

## TEACHING PROGRESSION

### Step 1: 3v0 Pass and Cut

Three players on the perimeter. Pass, cut to the basket, and fill the open spot. No defense. Focus on the habit of cutting after every pass.

### Step 2: 3v3 Pass and Cut

Add defense. Now the cutter has to read: if the defender follows, the cutter is open. If the defender stays, the perimeter is open. This is where basketball IQ develops.

### Step 3: 4v4 and 5v5

Expand to more players. The same rules apply, but spacing and timing become more important. Players must fill spots quickly to maintain spacing.

## COMMON MISTAKES

- Not cutting after every pass — kills the movement
- Cutting slowly — allows the defense to recover
- Not filling the open spot — breaks spacing
- Standing and watching after passing — the most common youth basketball mistake

> KEY TAKEAWAY | Pass, cut, and fill is the foundation of motion offense. After every pass, cut hard to the basket, then fill the open spot. This creates constant movement, forces defensive reactions, and develops basketball IQ. Teach it progressively from 3v0 to 5v5.
