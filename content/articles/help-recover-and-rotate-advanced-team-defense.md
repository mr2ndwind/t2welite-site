Once your players can guard the ball individually, the next step is teaching them to play defense as a team. Help, recover, and rotate is the foundation of team defense. This guide explains how to teach these concepts to teams that are ready for advanced defensive concepts.

## HELP-SIDE POSITIONING

When a player is more than one pass away from the ball, they are in help position. Help position means being positioned to help if the on-ball defender gets beat, while still being able to recover to your own player. The key principle is: see the ball and your player.

- One pass away: deny position, ready to help
- Two passes away: help position, in the lane area
- Three+ passes away: deep help, loaded toward the ball
- Always see the ball and your player — never turn your back to either

## RECOVERING

When help is given, the helper must recover to their player as soon as the threat is neutralized. Recovery is a sprint, not a jog. If recovery is slow, the offense gets an open shot.

## ROTATIONS

When the ball is moved, the entire defense rotates. Players adjust from deny to help or help to deny based on where the ball goes. The shell drill is the best tool for teaching rotations.

### Shell Drill Progression

- Start 4v4 with the ball stationary — teach positioning
- Add ball movement — teach rotations
- Add dribble penetration — teach help and recover
- Add cuts — teach communication and switching

## BALL-SCREEN DEFENSE

Ball screens are one of the most common offensive actions. Your team needs a consistent approach to defending them. The two most common coverages are:

- Hedge: the screener's defender steps out to slow the ball handler, then recovers
- Switch: the defenders switch assignments — simple but can create mismatches

Choose one coverage and teach it well rather than trying to install multiple coverages at once. Consistency in coverage is more important than variety.

> FOR ATHLETES | Communication is the most important part of team defense. If you see a screen coming, call it out. If you are helping, let your teammate know. Silent defense is slow defense.

> KEY TAKEAWAY | Advanced team defense is built on help-side positioning, quick recovery, coordinated rotations, and consistent ball-screen coverage. Use the shell drill to teach these concepts progressively. Communication is the glue that holds team defense together.
