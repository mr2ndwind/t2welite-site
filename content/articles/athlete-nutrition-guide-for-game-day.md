What an athlete eats on game day can have a real impact on how they feel and perform. While no meal will magically transform a player's ability, poor nutrition can leave an athlete feeling sluggish, cramping, or running out of energy in the second half. This guide provides general, practical nutrition guidance for game day. This is general education, not individualized medical advice. Athletes with specific dietary needs or health conditions should consult a qualified professional.

## THE NIGHT BEFORE: BUILD YOUR ENERGY RESERVES

The meal the night before a game matters more than many athletes realize. This is when your body builds the glycogen stores that fuel you the next day. Focus on a balanced meal with complex carbohydrates, lean protein, and plenty of fluids.

- Complex carbohydrates: brown rice, whole wheat pasta, sweet potatoes, quinoa
- Lean protein: grilled chicken, fish, lean beef, eggs
- Vegetables and fruits for vitamins and minerals
- Plenty of water throughout the evening

Avoid heavy, greasy, or unfamiliar foods the night before a game. This is not the time to try a new restaurant or eat something that might not sit well. Stick with foods your athlete is used to.

## PRE-GAME MEAL: 2-4 HOURS BEFORE TIP-OFF

The pre-game meal should be eaten 2 to 4 hours before the game starts. The goal is to top off energy stores without leaving food sitting heavy in the stomach. The meal should be higher in carbohydrates, moderate in protein, and low in fat and fiber, which slow digestion.

### Good Pre-Game Meal Options

- Oatmeal with fruit and a small amount of nut butter
- Turkey sandwich on whole wheat bread with a piece of fruit
- Grilled chicken with rice and steamed vegetables
- Pasta with a light tomato sauce and a small portion of lean protein

> FOR PARENTS | Every athlete digests food differently. Pay attention to what meals have worked well before good games and what has caused stomach discomfort. Over time, you will learn what your athlete tolerates best.

## PRE-GAME SNACK: 30-60 MINUTES BEFORE

If the game is less than an hour away, a small, easily digestible snack can provide a quick energy boost. Keep it simple and carbohydrate-focused.

- A banana
- A handful of pretzels or crackers
- A small granola bar
- A piece of toast with a thin spread of jam

Avoid high-fat, high-fiber, or heavy foods in the hour before the game. They take too long to digest and can cause discomfort during play.

## HYDRATION: THE MOST OVERLOOKED PART OF GAME DAY

Dehydration is one of the most common causes of fatigue and cramping in youth athletes. By the time an athlete feels thirsty, they are already mildly dehydrated. Hydration should start the day before the game and continue through game day.

- Drink water consistently throughout the day before the game
- Sip water in the hours leading up to tip-off
- Drink water during timeouts and halftime
- For games lasting longer than an hour or in hot conditions, a sports drink with electrolytes can help replace sodium lost through sweat

> FOR ATHLETES | If your urine is dark yellow before the game, you are not hydrated enough. Aim for pale yellow. Start drinking water early in the day, not just when you arrive at the gym.

## POST-GAME RECOVERY: REFUEL AND REBUILD

Within 30 to 60 minutes after the game, athletes should eat a meal or snack that combines carbohydrates and protein. This helps replenish energy stores and supports muscle recovery. This is especially important for athletes who play in tournaments with multiple games in a day.

- Chocolate milk (a popular choice that provides both carbs and protein)
- A turkey or chicken sandwich
- Greek yogurt with fruit and granola
- A smoothie with fruit, protein, and a banana

> KEY TAKEAWAY | Game day nutrition is about consistency and timing. Eat a balanced meal the night before, top off with carbohydrates 2-4 hours before the game, hydrate throughout, and refuel within an hour after. Avoid trying anything new on game day.
