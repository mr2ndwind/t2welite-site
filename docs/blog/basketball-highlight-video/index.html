What Should Be in a Basketball Highlight Video?

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 4 min read

A highlight video is often a college coach's first impression of a basketball player. Coaches see hundreds of highlight reels, and they often decide within the first minute whether they will keep watching. A well-made highlight video can open doors. A poorly made one can close them. This guide explains what to include, what to avoid, and how to make your skills easy for coaches to evaluate.

MAKE IT EASY TO IDENTIFY YOU QUICKLY

College coaches are busy. They may watch dozens of highlight reels in a sitting. Make it easy for them to know who you are and what they are looking at.

  • Start with a brief intro slide: name, height, position, graduation year, school, and contact info
  • Keep the intro under 5 seconds — coaches want to see basketball, not graphics
  • If possible, use an arrow or circle to identify yourself at the start of each clip
  • Include your jersey number in the intro so coaches can find you

SHOW USEFUL CLIPS QUICKLY

Lead with your best clips. Coaches often decide within the first minute whether they will continue watching. Put your most impressive, representative plays first. Do not save the best for last — the coach may never get there.

WHAT TO INCLUDE

Shooting

Show a variety of shots — catch and shoot, shooting off the dribble, free throws, and three-pointers. Coaches want to see your shooting form and range.

Passing

Include passes that show court vision and decision making. Good passing clips demonstrate basketball IQ, not just the pass itself.

Decision Making

Include clips where you make the right read — when to shoot, pass, or drive. Coaches value players who make good decisions under pressure.

Defense

Show on-ball defense, help defense, and rebounding. Many highlight reels ignore defense, so including it can set you apart.

Rebounding

Show rebounds in traffic, outlet passes, and effort plays. Rebounding demonstrates toughness and positioning.

Transition Play

Include clips of you in transition — pushing the ball, filling lanes, and making plays in the open court.

Athleticism

Show clips that display your athleticism — speed, jumping, and body control. But do not make the entire video just dunks and athletic plays.

Position-Specific Strengths

Include clips that show the skills relevant to your position. Point guards should show ball handling and playmaking. Posts should show footwork, positioning, and finishing.

WHAT TO AVOID

  • Excessive intros or graphics that delay the basketball content
  • Music with explicit lyrics or distracting edits
  • Repeating the same type of play over and over — variety matters
  • Workout footage with no game context — coaches want to see you compete
  • Overly long videos — 3-5 minutes is plenty
  • Slow motion or effects that make it hard to evaluate your actual speed

BE ACCURATE WITH INFORMATION

Be honest about your statistics, height, and other information. Coaches verify this information, and discrepancies can end their interest immediately. If you are listed at 6'2" and show up to a visit at 5'10", the coach will notice. Accuracy builds trust.

FOR ATHLETESYour highlight video is a tool to get a coach to watch you play in person. It is not the final evaluation. Be accurate, be concise, and show a variety of skills. The goal is to earn a closer look, not to make a highlight reel that tricks anyone.
KEY TAKEAWAYA basketball highlight video should be 3-5 minutes, lead with your best clips, and show a variety of skills — shooting, passing, decision making, defense, rebounding, transition, athleticism, and position-specific strengths. Make it easy to identify you, avoid distractions, and be accurate with all information.
