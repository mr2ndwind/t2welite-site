Teaching Five-Out Motion Offense to Young Players

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 9 min read · Sep 9, 2026

Five-out motion offense is one of the best systems for developing young basketball players. It places all five players on the perimeter and teaches them to read the defense, cut, pass, screen, and fill space based on principles rather than memorized plays. This guide explains how to teach five-out motion offense progressively.

WHY FIVE-OUT MOTION WORKS FOR YOUTH PLAYERS

Five-out motion develops basketball IQ. Instead of running set plays where players follow a script, motion offense forces players to read the defense and make decisions. Every player learns every position, which creates more complete players. It also spreads the floor, which gives young athletes space to drive, cut, and operate.

PHASE 1: SPACING

Before teaching any movement, players need to understand spacing. In five-out, the five spots are the two corners, two wings, and the top of the key. Players should be wide enough to create driving lanes but close enough to make crisp passes.

  • Teach the five spots: top, two wings, two corners
  • Emphasize being 'wide and ready'
  • If two players are in the same area, spacing is broken
  • Practice filling spots without the ball first

PHASE 2: PASS AND CUT

The first movement rule is simple: after you pass, cut to the basket. This creates movement, forces the defense to react, and gives the cutter a chance to score. After the cut, the player fills the open spot.

FOR ATHLETESCut hard to the basket every time. Even if you do not get the ball, your cut creates space for teammates and forces the defense to move. A lazy cut helps no one.

PHASE 3: READ THE DEFENSE

Once players understand pass and cut, add the read. When a player catches on the wing, they read the defense: drive if open, pass if pressured, or hold if the defense is balanced. This is where basketball IQ develops.

  • If the defender plays off: shoot or drive
  • If the defender overplays: backdoor cut
  • If the defense is balanced: pass and cut to maintain movement

PHASE 4: ADDING SCREENS

Once pass and cut is smooth, add down screens and back screens. A player without the ball can screen for a teammate, who reads the screen and cuts. This adds layers without complexity.

COMMON MISTAKES WHEN TEACHING MOTION

  • Teaching all phases at once — build progressively
  • Allowing players to stand — motion requires constant movement
  • Not enforcing spacing — without spacing, nothing works
  • Correcting every decision — let players learn from reads
KEY TAKEAWAYFive-out motion offense develops basketball IQ by teaching players to read and react rather than memorize plays. Teach it progressively: spacing first, then pass and cut, then reading the defense, then adding screens. Enforce spacing and constant movement at every phase.
