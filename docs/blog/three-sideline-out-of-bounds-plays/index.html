Three Sideline Out-of-Bounds Plays Every Coach Should Know

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 7 min read · Sep 4, 2026

Sideline out-of-bounds situations are often overlooked in practice, but they create valuable scoring opportunities — especially in close games. Here are three simple sideline out-of-bounds plays that youth basketball coaches can teach quickly and use effectively.

PLAY 1: DIAMOND — POST SLIP

Alignment

Four players form a diamond: one at the ball-side elbow, one at the opposite elbow, one in the ball-side corner, and one at the top of the key.

Movement

  • The ball-side elbow player screens for the corner player, who cuts to the wing (first option)
  • The screener slips to the basket (second option)
  • The top-of-key player drifts to the opposite wing (safety option)

Coaching Cues

The slip is the key. If the defense focuses on the screen, the screener is open cutting to the basket. Sell the screen, then slip.

PLAY 2: LINE — BACK SCREEN AND POP

Alignment

Players line up along the free-throw line extended, spaced evenly. The inbounder is on the sideline.

Movement

  • The first player in line sets a back screen for the second player, who cuts to the basket (first option)
  • The screener pops to the perimeter (second option)
  • The remaining players fill the spacing (safety option)

Coaching Cues

Back screens are effective but must be legal. Make sure the screener sets position before the cutter makes contact. A moving screen is a foul and kills the play.

PLAY 3: BOX — CROSS SCREEN

Alignment

Two players at the elbows, two at the low blocks — a box set facing the sideline.

Movement

  • The ball-side low-block player cross-screens for the opposite low-block player, who cuts to the ball-side block (first option)
  • The ball-side elbow pops to the wing (second option)
  • The opposite elbow fills the top (safety option)

Coaching Cues

The cross screen creates a mismatch or an open cut. The cutter should come off the screen tight — shoulder to shoulder — so the defender cannot slip through.

KEY TAKEAWAYThree sideline out-of-bounds plays — Diamond Post Slip, Line Back Screen, and Box Cross Screen — provide reliable scoring options. Each has a first, second, and safety option. Teach the alignments, practice the cuts, and keep the cues simple.
