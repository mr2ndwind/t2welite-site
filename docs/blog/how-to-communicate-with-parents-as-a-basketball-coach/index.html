How Basketball Coaches Can Communicate With Parents

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 8 min read · Sep 29, 2026

Parent communication is one of the most challenging parts of coaching youth basketball. Done well, it creates support and trust. Done poorly, it creates conflict and frustration. This guide explains how basketball coaches can communicate effectively with parents throughout the season.

SET EXPECTATIONS BEFORE THE SEASON

The most important communication happens before the first practice. A preseason parent meeting sets the tone for the entire season. Cover your philosophy, expectations, playing time policy, schedule, and how you handle feedback.

  • Coaching philosophy and development approach
  • Practice and game schedules
  • Playing time policy — be honest about how it works
  • Communication channels — how and when parents should contact you
  • Behavioral expectations for parents and athletes
FOR PARENTSA coach who holds a preseason meeting and clearly explains their philosophy is showing respect for your family's commitment. If a coach avoids explaining their approach, that is worth noting.

BE PROACTIVE, NOT REACTIVE

Do not wait for a problem to communicate. Regular updates — a weekly email or message — keep parents informed and reduce anxiety. When parents know what is happening, they are less likely to assume the worst.

HOW TO HANDLE PLAYING TIME CONVERSATIONS

Playing time is the most common source of parent-coach conflict. Be clear about your policy from the start. If playing time is earned, explain what earns it. If it is equal, say so. When a parent asks about playing time, listen, acknowledge their concern, and explain your decision without being defensive.

CREATE A FEEDBACK PROCESS

Tell parents how and when to give feedback. A good rule: no feedback immediately before or after games — emotions are high on both sides. Schedule a time to talk if a parent has a concern. This protects the athlete and keeps conversations productive.

WHAT TO AVOID

  • Discussing other players with parents — never compare athletes
  • Being defensive when challenged — listen first
  • Promising playing time you cannot guarantee
  • Avoiding difficult conversations — they only get harder later
KEY TAKEAWAYEffective parent communication starts before the season with clear expectations, continues with proactive updates, and includes a structured process for feedback. Be honest about playing time, never discuss other players, and address concerns calmly and directly.
