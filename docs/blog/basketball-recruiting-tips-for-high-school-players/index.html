Basketball Recruiting Tips for High School Players: Getting on a Coach's Radar

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 4 min read

The basketball recruiting process can feel overwhelming for high school players and their families. There is no single path to playing at the next level, and much of the process is outside an athlete's control. But there are concrete steps you can take to put yourself in the best position to be seen and evaluated by college coaches. This guide covers practical recruiting tips for high school basketball players.

TAKE CARE OF ACADEMICS FIRST

This is the tip that matters most and the one athletes most often overlook. College coaches will not invest in a player who cannot qualify academically. Your GPA and test scores determine what levels you are eligible for. A strong academic record does not just keep doors open — it opens more of them. Coaches at academically competitive programs will not consider players who do not meet their standards, no matter how talented they are.

FOR PARENTSHelp your athlete understand that academics are part of recruiting from day one of high school. A strong GPA gives your athlete options. A weak GPA takes options away, even for talented players.

BUILD A REALISTIC HIGHLIGHT REEL

College coaches see hundreds of highlight reels. Yours needs to make an impression quickly. Keep it short — 3 to 5 minutes is plenty. Lead with your best clips. Coaches often decide within the first minute whether they will keep watching.

What to Include

  • A brief intro slide with your name, height, position, graduation year, school, and contact info
  • Clips that show a variety of skills, not just scoring
  • Game footage, not just individual workouts — coaches want to see you compete
  • Clips that show basketball IQ: passing, help defense, off-ball movement
  • Quality video that clearly shows your number and the play

What to Avoid

  • Music with explicit lyrics or distracting edits
  • Repeating the same type of play over and over
  • Workout footage with no game context
  • Overly long reels that coaches will not finish

BE PROACTIVE ABOUT CONTACTING COACHES

Waiting to be discovered is not a strategy. Most college programs, especially at the Division II, Division III, NAIA, and junior college levels, do not have the budget to recruit nationally. They rely on athletes who reach out to them. Put together a list of schools that fit your academic, athletic, and personal goals, and start reaching out.

How to Reach Out

  • Send a short, professional email to the coaching staff
  • Include your highlight reel link, not an attachment
  • Mention why you are interested in their specific program
  • Include your upcoming game and tournament schedule
  • Follow up periodically with updates on your season

Do not send the same generic email to every coach. Take the time to learn about each program and personalize your message. Coaches can tell when an athlete has done their research.

PLAY WHERE COACHES CAN SEE YOU

Exposure matters, but it has to be the right kind. Playing in high-profile tournaments can help, but only if college coaches are actually attending. Talk to your high school and club coaches about which events draw college coaches at your level. A well-run showcase with coaches from schools that fit your ability is more valuable than a big-name tournament with no one watching who fits you.

FOR ATHLETESYour club coach and high school coach are your best advocates. Keep them informed about which schools you are interested in. They often have relationships with college coaches and can make a call that gets your film watched.

KEEP DEVELOPING YOUR GAME

Recruiting is a process that plays out over years, not weeks. While you are waiting for interest to build, keep getting better. The player you are as a freshman is not the player you will be as a senior. Coaches recruit based on trajectory, not just current ability. An athlete who is clearly improving year over year is more attractive than one who peaked early.

Focus on the parts of your game that need work. If you are a guard who cannot shoot, spend the offseason fixing that. If you are a post player who cannot move laterally, work on your footwork. The athletes who get recruited are the ones who address their weaknesses, not the ones who only practice their strengths.

KEY TAKEAWAYRecruiting is a proactive process. Prioritize academics, build a concise highlight reel, reach out to coaches at schools that fit you, play in front of the right people, and keep developing your game. The athletes who take control of the process give themselves the best chance.
