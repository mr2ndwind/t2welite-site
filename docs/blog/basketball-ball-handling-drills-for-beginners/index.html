Basketball Ball Handling Drills for Beginners: Build Your Foundation

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 4 min read

Ball handling is the foundation of every basketball player's game. Whether you are a point guard who runs the offense or a post player who wants to be more versatile, the ability to control the basketball with confidence changes how you play. This guide covers beginner-friendly ball handling drills that build the foundation every player needs.

WHY BALL HANDLING COMES FIRST

Before you can shoot off the dribble, beat your defender, or run a fast break, you need to be comfortable with the ball in your hands. Beginners often rush to advanced moves before they have basic control. The result is turnovers, tense dribbling, and a game that looks rushed. Mastering the fundamentals first makes every advanced skill easier to learn later.

STATIONARY DRILLS TO BUILD CONTROL

Start with stationary drills. They let you focus entirely on the ball without worrying about defenders or movement. Spend 10-15 minutes on these before any moving drills.

1. Pound Dribbles

Stand in an athletic stance and dribble the ball as hard as you can with your right hand, keeping it at knee height. Do 50 reps, then switch to your left hand. Pound dribbles build wrist strength and ball control while teaching you to dribble with force rather than patting the ball softly.

2. Side-to-Side Crossovers

Dribble the ball back and forth in front of you from your right hand to your left hand. Keep the dribble low and wide. Start slow, then increase speed as you get comfortable. Focus on keeping the ball below your waist and your eyes up.

3. Figure-Eight Dribbling

Dribble the ball in a figure-eight pattern between your legs. This drill builds coordination and gets you comfortable moving the ball around your body. It is challenging for beginners, so start slowly and prioritize control over speed.

FOR ATHLETESKeep your eyes up. If you have to look at the ball, you are not ready to move on. Practice dribbling while watching a wall or a coach. The ball should be controlled by feel, not sight.

MOVING DRILLS TO ADD GAME REALISM

Once you are comfortable with stationary drills, add movement. These drills simulate game situations where you are dribbling while moving down the court.

1. Dribble Walks

Walk the length of the court while dribbling with your right hand, then back with your left. Focus on keeping the ball under control and your eyes up. This seems simple, but it builds the habit of dribbling while in motion.

2. Zig-Zag Dribbling

Set up cones in a zig-zag pattern. Dribble to each cone, changing hands at each one. Plant your outside foot and push off sharply. This drill combines ball handling with the change of direction you need in games.

3. Stop-and-Go Dribbling

Dribble forward at a jog, then stop suddenly and stay in a low stance for two seconds before starting again. This teaches you to control the ball while changing speeds, which is how you keep defenders off balance.

HOW TO STRUCTURE YOUR BALL HANDLING PRACTICE

  • 10 minutes of stationary drills (pound dribbles, crossovers, figure-eights)
  • 10 minutes of moving drills (dribble walks, zig-zags, stop-and-go)
  • 5 minutes of weak-hand only dribbling
  • Practice 4-5 days per week for best results
FOR PARENTSBall handling is one of the few basketball skills that can be improved at home with just a ball and a flat surface. Encourage your athlete to practice in short, daily sessions rather than one long weekly session. Consistency builds feel.
KEY TAKEAWAYBall handling is built through daily, focused repetition of fundamental drills. Start stationary, add movement, and always keep your eyes up. Master the basics before attempting advanced moves.
