ATHLETE NUTRITION

What to Eat Before a Game

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 3 min read

What an athlete eats before a game can affect how they feel and perform. No meal will magically transform ability, but poor pre-game nutrition can leave an athlete feeling sluggish, heavy, or out of energy. This guide provides simple, practical pre-game nutrition strategies. This is general education, not individualized medical advice. Athletes with specific dietary needs should consult a qualified professional.

THE PRE-GAME MEAL: 2-4 HOURS BEFORE COMPETITION

The main pre-game meal should be eaten 2 to 4 hours before tip-off. The goal is to top off energy stores without leaving food sitting heavy in the stomach. The meal should be higher in carbohydrates, moderate in protein, and lower in fat and fiber, which slow digestion.

Good Pre-Game Meal Options

  • Oatmeal with fruit and a small amount of nut butter
  • Turkey sandwich on whole wheat bread with a piece of fruit
  • Grilled chicken with rice and steamed vegetables
  • Pasta with a light tomato sauce and a small portion of lean protein
  • Scrambled eggs with toast and a banana

The exact meal matters less than the principles: carbohydrates for energy, moderate protein, low fat and fiber, and foods the athlete is familiar with. Game day is not the time to try new foods.

THE PRE-GAME SNACK: 30-60 MINUTES BEFORE

If the game is less than an hour away, a small, easily digestible snack can provide a quick energy boost. Keep it simple and carbohydrate-focused.

  • A banana
  • A handful of pretzels or crackers
  • A small granola bar
  • A piece of toast with a thin spread of jam
  • Applesauce or fruit cup

Avoid high-fat, high-fiber, or heavy foods in the hour before the game. They take too long to digest and can cause discomfort during play.

WHY CARBOHYDRATES MATTER

Carbohydrates are the primary fuel for high-intensity exercise. Your body stores them as glycogen in the muscles and liver, and these stores get depleted during exercise. Pre-game carbohydrates top off those stores so the athlete has fuel available when the game starts. Without adequate carbohydrates, athletes may feel fatigued earlier in the game.

PROTEIN AND FLUIDS

Protein supports muscle repair and can help with satiety, but it is not the primary fuel for performance. Include a moderate amount of protein in the pre-game meal. Fluids are equally important. Dehydration is one of the most common causes of fatigue and cramping in young athletes. Start hydrating early in the day, not just when you arrive at the gym.

FOR PARENTSEvery athlete digests food differently. Pay attention to what meals have worked well before good games and what has caused stomach discomfort. Over time, you will learn what your athlete tolerates best. Stick with familiar foods on game day.

WHAT TO AVOID BEFORE A GAME

  • Heavy, greasy, or fried foods
  • New or unfamiliar foods
  • High-fiber foods that can cause digestive discomfort
  • Large meals close to game time
  • Sugary drinks or energy drinks that can cause a crash
KEY TAKEAWAYPre-game nutrition is about timing and familiarity. Eat a carbohydrate-focused meal 2-4 hours before the game, add a light snack 30-60 minutes before if needed, hydrate throughout the day, and avoid new or heavy foods. Individual tolerance varies, so learn what works for your athlete.
