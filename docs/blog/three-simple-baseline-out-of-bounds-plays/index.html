Three Simple Baseline Out-of-Bounds Plays

By Coach Corey Scyffore · 7 min read · Aug 31, 2026

Baseline out-of-bounds situations happen multiple times per game. Having a few simple, reliable plays gives your team confidence and scoring opportunities without spending hours of practice on complex sets. Here are three baseline out-of-bounds plays designed for youth basketball teams.

PLAY 1: BOX SET — SCREEN THE SCREENER

Alignment

Four players form a box near the lane — two at the free-throw line elbows and two at the low blocks. The inbounder stands out of bounds on the baseline.

Movement

  • The player at the top of the box (elbow) screens across for the player at the opposite elbow
  • The screened player cuts to the basket looking for a layup (first option)
  • The screener then pops to the wing for a shot (second option)
  • The low-block players box out and look for a rebound or kick-out (safety option)

Coaching Cues

Set a legal screen with good angle. The cutter should read the defense — if the defense switches, the screener is open. If the defense stays, the cutter has the layup.

PLAY 2: STACK — SPLIT AND CUT

Alignment

Two players stack on the low block, one on each side of the lane. One player stands at the free-throw line, and one on the wing.

Movement

  • The stacked players split — one cuts to the basket, one pops to the corner (first and second options)
  • The free-throw line player screens down for the wing player, who cuts to the top (third option)
  • The screener slips to the basket (safety option if defense overplays)

Coaching Cues

The split should be sharp and simultaneous. If the defense guards the basket cut, the corner shot is open. If the defense covers both, look for the player cutting to the top.

PLAY 3: SPREAD — ISOLATE THE BEST FINISHER

Alignment

Spread four players across the perimeter — two in the corners and two on the wings. Your best finisher starts on the ball-side low block.

Movement

  • The finisher cuts hard to the basket (first option)
  • If denied, the finisher pops to the wing and the opposite wing cuts backdoor (second option)
  • The ball-side corner player drifts to the basket for a rebound (safety option)

Coaching Cues

This play is simple and hard to defend if the cut is sharp. The spread alignment clears the lane for the finisher. If the defense cheats toward the basket, the backdoor cut is available.

FOR PARENTSSimple plays work at every level. The best out-of-bounds plays have clear first, second, and safety options so players always know where to look. Complexity does not equal effectiveness.
KEY TAKEAWAYThree simple baseline out-of-bounds plays — Box Screen-the-Screener, Stack Split, and Spread Isolate — give your team reliable scoring options. Each has a first option, second option, and safety option. Teach the cues, practice the cuts, and keep it simple.
